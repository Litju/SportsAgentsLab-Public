## Findings

| Contract topic | What primary sources support | What remains an engineering decision |
|---|---|---|
| **Sample-rate adequacy** | Street et al. report impulse-method jump-height underestimation up to 4.4% below 1,080 Hz; their full-text result reports values above 1,000 Hz within 0.1% of reference. Hori et al. found common peak force/power/velocity measures reliable down to 200 Hz depending on purpose, but peak RFD and time-to-peak-power were exceptions. These are metric- and signal-processing-dependent findings, not a universal minimum. | Declare the supported metric pack first, then its minimum qualified rate. Record anti-aliasing/filter characteristics and whether the rate is acquisition or post-resampling. Do not infer adequacy from rate alone. |
| **Quiet standing / body weight** | Owen et al.'s criterion protocol uses mean vertical ground-reaction force during **1 s of quiet standing immediately before the jump**; the same abstract identifies body-weight determination as one of the variables that materially affects validity. Street et al. found shortened averaging increases random error. NIST distinguishes weight as force (N) from mass (kg). | Define the baseline window, required stillness/quality checks, whether all plates/channels are summed, the gravity constant used for `mass = body_weight / g`, and the treatment of external load. Keep `body_weight_N` and `body_mass_kg` semantically distinct. No stillness threshold is adopted here. |
| **Movement onset / phase events** | Owen et al. specify a force-deviation rule and an integration start offset in their criterion protocol. Pinto & Callaghan compared 5×SD, reverse-scanning, and first-derivative onset methods against manual onsets, demonstrating that onset detection is a method choice requiring validation. Harry et al. show force-platform data can identify unloading, eccentric, and concentric phases when definitions are tied to center-of-mass/joint mechanics. Sahrom et al. report that force-time data alone do not accurately locate the first joint-flexion event; the yank-time signal or motion analysis changes that boundary. | Freeze separate definitions for signal start, movement onset, braking/velocity-zero, propulsion, take-off, landing, and integration bounds. Treat published values such as 5×SD, 5 N, 10 N, or 30 ms as source-specific examples, not ML-109 defaults. State whether events are sample indices, interpolated times, or interval boundaries. |
| **Deterministic trapezoidal integration** | Owen et al. found Simpson's rule or the trapezoidal rule compatible with <1% peak-power error in their optimized protocol. Force-derived velocity follows `a_i = (F_i - BW) / m`, then numerical integration; jump height from take-off velocity is `h = v_takeoff² / (2g)`. ISO/IEC 60559:2020 states that conforming numerical results are uniquely determined by input values, operation sequence, and destination formats. | Fix the exact trapezoid convention, timestamp/`dt` source, endpoint inclusion, accumulation order, floating-point format, non-finite handling, and output rounding. “Deterministic” requires these choices plus a replay oracle; the standard does not choose the biomechanics or event boundaries. Round only after calculation if reproducibility is required. |
| **Impulse–momentum jump height** | Linthorne describes jump height from the impulse–momentum theorem applied to the force-time curve. The force-platform take-off-velocity method measures COM height from take-off to apex; Moir's primary comparison shows that time-in-air, take-off-velocity, and take-off-plus-pre-take-off-displacement are different definitions and produce different values. | Name the metric explicitly (for example, `jump_height_takeoff_to_apex`) and preserve its reference state. Do not silently call it standing-to-apex height. Fix `g`, baseline subtraction, take-off event, and whether pre-take-off COM displacement is excluded or separately reported. |
| **Known limitations / quality** | Vanrenterghem et al. identify body-mass measurement, take-off determination, integration frequency, and initial conditions as error sources. Street et al. show filtering and sampling can dominate computational-method error. Sahrom et al. show early joint-flexion onset is not recoverable from force-time alone. Anicic et al. found only 24 of 45 candidate CMJ variables met their study's reliability criterion, so a large metric pack is not automatically defensible. | Make unsupported phase/metric claims explicit, retain failure reasons, and qualify the tested population/protocol. Define which bad baseline, missing/ambiguous event, non-finite sample, saturation, or out-of-scope technique conditions reject a trial. These are product-quality decisions, not universal literature thresholds. |

## Contract implications (evidence vs. decision)

**Literature-supported rules to carry forward:** preserve raw declared rate and signal-processing metadata; compute body weight from a documented quiet-standing segment; distinguish force from mass; use an explicitly documented net-force integration; identify the jump-height reference state; and report method/error limitations alongside derived metrics.

**Not supplied by the literature as a universal rule:** one minimum sample rate for every metric; one movement-onset/take-off/landing threshold; one quiet-standing duration or stillness tolerance for every device; one gravity constant/rounding policy; or a universally reliable set of phase metrics. ML-109 should freeze these as scoped engineering choices with replay evidence and source-specific rationale, not as claims that the literature has established a single standard.

## Primary sources and exact claim locators

1. [Hori et al. (2009), DOI 10.1519/JSC.0b013e3181a00ca2](https://doi.org/10.1519/JSC.0b013e3181a00ca2), **Abstract: Methods, Results, Conclusion** — resampling comparison from 500 Hz to 25 Hz; metric-specific reliability and sampling-rate findings.
2. [Street et al. (2001), DOI 10.1123/jab.17.1.43](https://doi.org/10.1123/jab.17.1.43), **Abstract; pp. 43–54; full-text Results pp. 49 and 54** — sampling/filter/body-weight/integration error analysis and final error check.
3. [Owen et al. (2014), DOI 10.1519/JSC.0000000000000311](https://doi.org/10.1519/JSC.0000000000000311), **Abstract: protocol specification and conclusion** — 1,000 Hz criterion protocol, 1 s quiet standing, trapezoidal/Simpson integration, and onset rule. The journal abstract reports 30 ms; an earlier conference abstract reports 10 ms, so the version must be checked before adoption: [conference record](https://ojs.ub.uni-konstanz.de/cpa/article/view/3193/2995).
4. [Linthorne (2001), DOI 10.1119/1.1397460](https://doi.org/10.1119/1.1397460), **Abstract; pp. 1198–1204** — flight-time, impulse–momentum, and work–energy formulations.
5. [Moir (2008), DOI 10.1080/10913670802349766](https://doi.org/10.1080/10913670802349766), **Abstract; pp. 207–218** — distinct time-in-air, take-off-velocity, and take-off-plus-pre-take-off-displacement height definitions.
6. [Vanrenterghem et al. (2001), DOI 10.1080/00140130118100](https://doi.org/10.1080/00140130118100), **Abstract; pp. 814–818** — body mass, take-off, integration frequency, and initial-condition error sources.
7. [Harry et al. (2020), DOI 10.1249/MSS.0000000000002197](https://doi.org/10.1249/MSS.0000000000002197), **Abstract: Purpose, Methods, Results, Conclusion** — force-platform phase definitions tied to joint power and COM velocity.
8. [Pinto & Callaghan (2023), DOI 10.1123/jab.2022-0111](https://doi.org/10.1123/jab.2022-0111), **Abstract; Journal of Applied Biomechanics 39(2), pp. 118–123** — comparison of automated onset-detection families against manual onset.
9. [Sahrom et al. (2020), DOI 10.1098/rsos.192093](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsos.192093), **§5.1 and §6** — force-time limitation for first joint-flexion onset and alternative yank-time event interpretation.
10. [Anicic et al. (2023), DOI 10.3390/life13010190](https://doi.org/10.3390/life13010190), **§2.3 Data processing; Abstract; §4 Discussion** — one published example of baseline/event thresholds and the reliability result for 45 candidate metrics; not a universal threshold authority.

## Official metrology/computation references

- [ISO 5725-1:2023](https://www.iso.org/standard/69418.html), **Preview abstract** — trueness/precision study principles for continuous measurement results, including calculated results; it does not prescribe CMJ formulas.
- [JCGM 100:2008 (BIPM)](https://www.bipm.org/en/doi/10.59161/jcgm100-2008e), **Abstract** — general rules for evaluating and expressing measurement uncertainty.
- [ISO/IEC 60559:2020](https://www.iso.org/standard/80985.html), **Preview abstract** — floating-point formats/operations and the dependence of numerical results on inputs, operation sequence, and destination formats.
- [NIST SP 811](https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.SP.811e2008), **§8.3 “Weight” and SI force/mass guidance** — units and the force-versus-mass distinction; use the PDF locator when implementing the contract.
